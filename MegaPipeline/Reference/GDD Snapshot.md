## Mechanic cards

### Action states and movement

Trigger / eligibility: Shoot and Absorb held states; Hop while overhead, dash while falling, Ride while overhead inside a current. Default bindings are listed below.

Only unspecified mode-change state remains OPEN.

Feedback / unavailable input: enforce hop/dash eligibility and cooldown. Invalid-action feedback and finished cues remain OPEN. Apply the shared protection baseline; do not silently enable dash invulnerability.

### Health, absorption and weapons

Trigger / eligibility: each yellow shot absorbed while dropping heals 0.5 active health at the starting recovery stat, applied to the active life first. Once it is full, further eligible yellow healing progressively rebuilds an empty reserve life, without exceeding maximum lives. The life hand shows progress toward restoring that reserve. Confirmed overflow rule: heal the active life first; unused healing from that same shot carries into rebuilding a spent reserve, and healing stops at maximum lives. Derived implementation default, not separately confirmed: one full reserve requires healing equal to the active life’s current maximum capacity; any remaining healing continues toward the next spent reserve. A clock-position example does not establish a new hand-angle mapping. Partial reserve progress after damage/retry and its remapping on capacity purchases/refunds remain OPEN. This does not revive Ella after final defeat. This is approximately16.7% of its initial three-hit capacity. Each applied health-recovery upgrade adds0.3 healing per absorbed shot; one upgrade heals0.8, approximately26.7% at initial capacity. Absorbed yellow energy visibly flows into the clock HUD, moving its hand toward III and restoring its lit arc; this visual fill does not set gameplay application timing. No ammunition or stored energy. Yellow absorption grants healing only, never XP.

Input / reward precedence: no ammunition or stored-energy channel. Yellow-shot absorption heals only and grants no XP. XP is earned only from kills / qualifying enemy defeats in Director’s Cut. Repair remains valid independently of rewards; full-health catches still register without granting health beyond the cap.

Interrupt / reset: repair cannot revive a defeated player. Both modes’ boss-phase retries restore full health; normal phase advancement carries health (section 5). Director’s Cut XP / upgrade loss and quit behavior is in section 4. Other reset / feedback details remain OPEN.

## Default controls and input behavior

DualSense labels are listed first; Xbox-style names identify corresponding logical controls. Verify each supported physical device and remapped layout. No hardware or runtime acceptance test is claimed.

### Core actions

Rise: release R2 / RT and L2 / LT (release Shoot and Absorb on keyboard / mouse). Umbrella overhead rises automatically; there is no separate fly button.

Shoot + descend: hold R2 / RT, left mouse or default J. Fires the equipped spell to the right, even while moving left.

Absorb + descend: hold L2 / LT, right mouse or default K. Capture eligible yellow shots within suction reach/cone and repair the umbrella while dropping. Red shots are not absorbable. Yellow absorption grants no XP.

Fast free fall: hold both triggers or both mouse buttons. Shooting and suction stop; descent is faster and enabled steering is reduced.

Overhead dodge hop: Cross / A or Space while rising / overhead. Falling dash: L1 / LB or Q backward; R1 / RB or E forward. Repeated taps work after cooldown without releasing Shoot / Absorb.

Context dash: Cross / A or Space while falling dashes backward if movement input is left, otherwise forward. This shortcut belongs to the remappable Hop action.

Ride wind: hold Triangle / Y or Left Shift inside a voluntary current while overhead. Release, shoot, absorb or free-fall to disengage; momentum decays. Voluntary currents are harmless; hostile gusts are separate hazards.

Vertical trim: left stick up / down, W / S or default Up / Down aliases. Up increases ascent or reduces descent; down does the reverse. Horizontal input: left stick left / right, A / D or default arrows. Direct horizontal steering is OFF in both normal modes, enabled only by tuning / experimental profile.

Right-stick flick selects immediately; hold to show the wheel, release to confirm. Defaults: 0.3-second hold, 0.45 dead zone, gameplay paused by wheel. Right stick selects spells; it does not aim. There is no default mouse aiming.

Expose four remappable spell actions. Stick selection uses separate wheel-axis actions; keyboard/mouse cannot bind analog axes. Mouse buttons and wheel directions may bind digital actions such as Next Spell. No fifth weapon action.

### Menus and remapping

Retry defeat: Cross / A or Enter retries the failed checkpoint / encounter, not a mid-run restart. Pause: Options / Menu, Escape or F1 recovery. Use F1 in editor play, where Escape may stop the session.

Title confirmation: Options / Menu, Cross / A, Enter, Space or left click after neutral / release; right mouse does not start. Main menu offers Start Game and Start Director’s Cut. Pause offers Continue, Settings and Quit, as specified in section 7.

Menu navigation: D-pad / left stick, arrows / WASD, Tab / Shift+Tab. Select: Cross / A, Enter, Space or click. Adjust: left / right. Back / resume: Circle / B, Options / Menu, Escape or F1, plus saved Pause binding. Fixed UI navigation is separate from gameplay remaps.

# 4. Gameplay systems

Yellow absorption, checkpoint passage/reload, traversal and other non-kill actions award no XP. A winning Director’s Cut run carries XP, level, weapons / unlocks and upgrades into the next run. Reallocate points only at the start of run 2 onward, never at checkpoints. On a level-checkpoint death retry, earned XP and character levels are retained; defeating an ordinary enemy again after retry can earn XP again. A boss-checkpoint retry instead restores the checkpoint’s XP/upgrades and their consistent derived progression state. Destroying boss parts is an eligible defeat and awards XP in Director’s Cut. Failed-attempt boss-part XP is lost and earned again only when that part is defeated again. The Keeper’s living departure alone adds no separate reward. ## nUMBeR ELLA

Ella was meant to die, but her mother refused to accept her fate. In desperation, she stole the clock hand marking Ella's final hour from the great owl, an ancient keeper of time and death. She fashioned the stolen hand into an umbrella whose umbra, a perfect shadow, could shield her daughter from the light of truth. By hiding Ella from that light, her mother protected her not only from death, but from the knowledge that her time had already run out. Ella knows nothing of the theft or her intended fate. She knows only that her mother gave her an umbrella to protect her from a terrifying owl.  At the edge of reality, where the living pass into the afterlife, Ella finds herself suspended above an endless abyss. The light of truth falls from the heavens like rain, weighing down everything whose time has come and drawing it toward the world beyond. Opening her umbrella creates an umbra that shields her from the light, allowing her to rise. Closing it exposes her to the light's weight, forcing her downward. The owl descends to reclaim the missing hand and restore the natural order. To Ella, he is a monster trying to kill her. To the owl, she is a life whose time has ended. As the umbrella deteriorates and the owl's attacks intensify, Ella struggles to remain aloft, unknowingly fighting to preserve the life her mother stole for her.  Eventually, the owl's pursuit ends, and he retreats into the darkness, leaving Ella alive but still suspended between worlds. She has survived, yet neither her fate nor the owl's intentions are resolved. Perhaps the owl has merely postponed his judgment, or perhaps something about Ella has challenged even his understanding of time. She still believes her mother saved her from a monster, unaware that the very umbrella protecting her also conceals the truth. Her mother defied death out of love, the owl seeks to restore what was taken, and Ella is left fighting for a life she doesn't know she was supposed to lose.
