Paint a new picture of the Keeper of Time, a large crownless mechanical owl, mysterious rather than monstrous, hovering and flapping slowly.

The picture is a 2x2 grid of four equal cells with no dividing lines between them, read left to right, top row then bottom row, each cell holding one whole owl. Every owl is in pure side view facing left, level camera, the same scale, filling about 80% of its cell height, with its body centre on the same spot in every cell and wide empty space around it. Body, head, clock, legs, tail and the three hanging mechanisms keep the same size and place in all four; only the wings move. Both wings show: the near wing in front, the far wing just behind and slightly darker.
Cell 1: both wings raised high above the back, tips pointing up.

The owl: a compact round torso centred on an aged clock face with Roman numerals, glass and exactly one long hand on a pivot. A round owl head with two flat tufted ear plates, a flat round facial disc, with a heavy brow, a short hooked beak, one dull, muted glass eye on the face, and a second small dull glass eye set on the very top of the head. Two very broad wings, each a fan of about eight long flat rigid blades of perforated, ridged sheet metal with straight cut edges and rivets, like a folding fan or a window shutter: no feathers, no barbs, no scale rows, no curved quills. Each wing joins the torso at a bevelled shoulder spar and hinge. Two short mechanical legs ending in exactly two clawed feet, hanging in the air with no ground. A plated tail. Clearly below the body hang exactly three separate mechanisms, spread along the length of the owl: under the beak at the far left hangs a small armillary sphere; under the middle of the belly, directly below the clock, hangs a ribbed cylinder with solid bronze walls; under the tail at the far right hangs a toothed gear ring. Each has one small dull eye on its underside. Each hangs on its own long thin straight chain that rises to the small glass eye on the very top of the head, three chains fanning down from that one point, the right one passing behind the torso to the gear; no chain loops or circles anywhere else. The mechanisms are far apart, hang below the legs' height, and none touches the wings, neck or feet.

Style: a dark etched storybook engraving for a 2D game sprite. Brown-black ink line #171614 with a heavy dark outer contour, sepia wash, fine cross-hatching and visible paper tooth on the owl only. Metal in mid-tone warm bronze and pewter steel, lit surfaces a warm tan bronze #B08D5E and pale steel #A89C8C, shaded surfaces #5C473E, with warm ivory highlights #EFE0C0, so most of the owl reads as mid-tone and light metal; only the ink lines and the deepest recesses are near black. Just inside the contour runs a thin rim light in warm candle-cream #FFF3D6 (leaning red-orange, never lemon or lime). Only the clock face glows, a soft gold #E9C46A. Every colour on the owl is a warm brown, gold or cream.

Background: one flat, perfectly even, pure green #00FF00 filling the whole picture edge to edge, clear of any marks, texture, gradient, smoke, text or shadow, with the owls floating freely on it.
