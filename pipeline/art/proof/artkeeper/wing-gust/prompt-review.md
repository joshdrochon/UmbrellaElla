# Prompt review: wing-gust (fresh reviewer session, 13 Nov; no paid call made)

VERDICT: change

Input checked: owl faces left, wings already raised (right wing tip at about x=980, y=210; root near x=480, y=330; wing length about 520 px, so the wing is only about 14 degrees above level). The top of the frame is 330 px above the root. "Lift far back and upward" would push the tips out of frame (anything over about 35 degrees leaves the top edge).

## Problems
1. Frame risk: "far back behind the body" from an already raised pose. Veo will likely lift the tips past the top edge. Cap the raise at about 30 degrees.
2. Contradictions that make Veo drift: "head lowering and staying fixed"; "folding tighter and fanning upward"; "body tilting back" then "lurching forward" (can rotate the owl out of side view); "draws in a breath of air" invites an air or glow effect, against the "no wind" rule.
3. Timing is loose. "About two seconds" gather, then nothing says when the flap lands. The cut to the 1.1 s warning plus launch needs the flap at a known time. Give seconds.
4. Front direction is only stated, not shown. The downstroke should end with wing tips swept past the beak to the left.
5. Design preservation lists parts but not which: name the armillary sphere, lantern and gear ring, the single hand, the dull eye (the idle prompt that worked did this).
6. Negatives are a long list. Keep one short sentence; the rest as positives (crisp, still air, still background).

## Edits
Replace the whole prompt body with:

> The mechanical owl hovers in strict side view, facing left, and winds up one great wing beat. For the first two seconds it gathers slowly: both rigid metal wings rise a little higher, to about thirty degrees above level, the riveted vanes pulling in slightly, the body sinking a touch back and to the right, the three hanging mechanisms (armillary sphere, lantern, gear ring) swinging gently back on their chains. The head stays level and keeps facing left. At about two seconds it strikes: in one fast, heavy downstroke both wings sweep down and forward until the wing tips pass in front of the beak, pointing down and to the left, the vanes spreading wide, the body surging a small step to the left, the hanging mechanisms swinging forward. In the last second the wings rise slowly back to the raised starting pose and the body settles. The beak, the head and the force of the beat always point left. The chest clock keeps its single long hand; the dim glass eye stays dull. Keep the exact etched ink-engraving style, the pale cream outline and the colours of the input; the owl keeps its shape, parts and size. Every edge stays crisp and the air around the owl stays perfectly still and empty. Static locked camera, no zoom, no pan, no cut; the owl stays near the same place in the frame and both wing tips stay inside the frame at every moment.

Notes on the edits:
- "about thirty degrees" and "pass in front of the beak" are the two measurable checks for the proof frames (tips in frame at the peak; front left at the end of the stroke).
- If the first run still clips the top edge, drop to "twenty degrees" before changing anything else.
- The tool-added green sentence and audio-off cover background and sound; no need to repeat them.
